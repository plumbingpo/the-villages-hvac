When Your System Fails in Jacksonville

When your system quits, how fast help arrives matters more than anything else. That's why we keep our schedule flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls typically see a technician within a day, and we prioritize a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat ahead of routine work.

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: warm air from the AC, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has frozen, a condensate drain overflowing into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. Nearly all of these come down to a part we already carry: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and standard fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or produces a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and call us. Those few minutes can prevent a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Jacksonville

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a system that runs for years and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and also shows higher bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement in peak season.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.
